Frequently asked questions

A word you do not know is probably in the glossary; anything missing here, ask with the feedback button (the speech bubble beside the light/dark switch).

What is CalCOFI.io, and how is it different from CalCOFI.org?

CalCOFI.io is CalCOFI’s data access platform, where you can view data on a map and download its datasets. The data is updated regularly as new cruise data comes in. CalCOFI.org is CalCOFI’s main website. It covers the program’s history, research, sampling methods and news.

Go to CalCOFI.org to learn about CalCOFI, and go to CalCOFI.io to explore and download the data. You can also get to each from the other: on CalCOFI.org, the Visualize data page under Data links to CalCOFI.io, and every CalCOFI.io page links back to CalCOFI.org in its header.

What’s on CalCOFI.io, and how do the parts fit together?

CalCOFI.io has one back end and several ways in. The back end is the integrated database: every dataset CalCOFI has brought in, translated into one shared set of stations, cruises, species and measurements (see What does “integrated” mean?). It is published as integrated database releases: dated copies, each with a DOI, never changed once published (see Is this the official copy, and how current is it?). Every way in reads the current release:

  • Dataset pages: one page per dataset, with what it is, who collected it, its years, how to cite it, its licence and its download.
  • The Explorer: one map across every dataset, for one species, one measurement, or one area, period, season or depth, with a download of what you choose.
  • Measurement pages: one page per measured quantity, such as temperature or oxygen, across the datasets that have it.
  • The R and Python packages: the same release, read from code.
  • This documentation: how the integrated database is built and how to use each part.

How do I download data?

Go to CalCOFI.io and choose a dataset. Download directly from the dataset’s own page. Each download is one zip file holding the data tables, a readme, how to cite the data, its licence and a dictionary of what every column means.

To get data for a specific area, time period, species or measurement, use the Explorer: choose what, when and where on the map, then press Download CSV.

There are other ways to get the same data. Each dataset page links to the original wherever its provider publishes it (“Original at …”), for the provider’s own version and format. The R and Python packages read the integrated database release directly from code (see Can I get the data in R or Python?).

Which datasets are available?

CalCOFI.io has data from CalCOFI’s ocean surveys, including physical oceanography, nutrients and chemistry, carbonate system, meteorology and sea state, phytoplankton, picoplankton and bacteria, zooplankton, euphausiids (krill), fish eggs and larvae, mesopelagic fish, seabirds and marine mammals, and genomics and eDNA. See the full dataset list for everything that’s available.

The current integrated database release holds 20 datasets. CalCOFI.io also lists 9 more datasets that aren’t available for download yet (listed as holdings). Their pages tell you where to find the data. More datasets are added over time.

Should I use the Explorer or a dataset’s page to download data?

It depends on what you need:

  • Dataset page: use this to browse a dataset, see an overview, find the original data source, or download the full dataset.
  • Explorer: use this to view data on a map, or to download only part of it, such as one region, time period, species or measurement.

What does “integrated” mean?

Each dataset, as published by its original source, has its own way of recording dates, locations, species names and units. CalCOFI.io standardizes every dataset into one shared format, with the same date/time format, station grid (CalCOFI line and station), scientific names (WoRMS, the World Register of Marine Species; ITIS for birds) and units.

“Integrated” data lets you use datasets together when they overlap in time and place, without your having to match anything up. For example, fish larvae from a net tow can be matched with a bottle temperature taken at the same station on the same cruise.

Keep in mind:

  • Depth: temperature is measured at many depths, while a net tow collects larvae across a range of depths. You’ll need to choose which temperature to use.
  • Years: each dataset covers different years. Check each one’s date range on its page.
  • Methods: each dataset uses its own sampling method, so read its page before comparing results.

To use datasets together, use the Query Builder, or use R or Python (see the code packages).

Is this the official copy, and how current is it?

Each dataset’s official version comes from its original source: every dataset page names the provider and links the original (“Original at …”). CalCOFI.io holds a copy of them all in integrated database releases. The current release is v2026.11.25, published 25 November 2026 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24541941); a new one comes out whenever the data change enough to matter, 21 times in the last 90 days. If you know a provider has newer data than a dataset page shows, tell us with the feedback button. Every release is listed in Releases.

How do I provide feedback on CalCOFI.io and its associated pages?

To report a problem (a value that looks wrong, a broken link, a page that confused you), use the feedback button on any CalCOFI.io page: the speech bubble at the top, or Send feedback at the foot of the page. It takes a picture of what you are looking at, lets you mark it up, and sends it with your note to the team. It is also filed as a public issue, so anyone can follow the fix; your email address is optional and never made public.

How do I cite the CalCOFI data?

Cite two things: each dataset you used (the people who collected it) and the integrated database release you got it from (the work that put them together). Both citations are on every dataset page under Cite, in every download (the CITATION file in the zip), and behind How to cite in the Explorer. The current release’s citation is:

CalCOFI (2026). CalCOFI Integrated Database, release v2026.11.25 [Data set]. Scripps Institution of Oceanography, NOAA Fisheries, and California Department of Fish and Wildlife. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24541941

Cite this data has every dataset’s citation and how to cite from R or Python.

What am I allowed to do with the data?

Each dataset has its own licence (its terms for reuse), and they aren’t all the same. You’ll find it on the dataset’s page and in its download.

In integrated database release v2026.11.25:

  • CC BY 4.0 (10 datasets): reuse freely, with credit.
  • CC0, public domain (3 datasets): reuse freely.
  • Provider’s own terms (5 datasets): see the dataset page.
  • No licence yet (2 datasets): ask data@calcofi.io before sharing the data.

The Licence filter on the list of datasets shows which is which.

Do I need to sign up or fill in a form to access the data?

No. Nothing on CalCOFI.io asks you to sign in, register or fill in a form before you look at data or download it: not the dataset pages, not their downloads, not the Explorer, not the R and Python packages. If you use the data in a paper, report, app or other product, we encourage you (it is never required) to register it. That credits the data providers and links your work from CalCOFI.io. In the Explorer, open Help and choose Register a product (how).

How do I contribute data?

To contribute a dataset, write to data@calcofi.io; Providing data to CalCOFI says what a dataset needs and what happens next, including that you see your data on a private copy of the site and say yes before anything is public.

Is there a tutorial?

Yes. The Explorer has a guided tour that walks you through it in the app itself: open Help at the top and choose Take the tour, or press ?. The Explore chapter is the written version of the tour and follows the same path, with a picture of what you should see at each stop.

Can I get the data in R or Python?

Yes. The calcofi4r package for R and calcofi4py for Python read the current integrated database release in a few lines, fetching only the rows you ask for rather than whole files, and cc_cite() in both gives the citations for exactly the datasets you used. Nothing needs a password or an account. Access the data shows how, with examples to copy.
